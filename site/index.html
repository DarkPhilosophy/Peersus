02 / TRUST, WITHOUT THE HYPE

Private connections.
Honest boundaries.

Encryption is important.
So is telling you what it doesn’t cover.

Encrypted device to device

File-transfer payloads use end-to-end encrypted QUIC connections. Each device has its own key, and a connection is made to a key, not to an address. When a direct path is unavailable, an optional relay can carry traffic that is already encrypted between the two devices: by design it cannot read your files, but it can observe connection metadata. You can turn relays off in the settings. There is no Peersus server in this picture, and no account.

Not always offline. Not risk-free.

Connectivity depends on your network and configuration. Peersus has not received an independent security audit. Trust the devices you pair, keep backups, and verify important files after transfer.

Plugins are a separate decision

Clipboard integration is under development, with Android foreground-app restrictions. Optional cloud upload is also under development: a provider may store readable files. Neither is a promise of automatic background sync or encrypted cloud storage.

      06 / BEFORE YOU ASK

      The useful details.

      Is Peersus free?

      Yes. The application is Apache-2.0 free software. Support is voluntary and unlocks nothing. Third-party network or payment services may have their own costs and terms.

      Can I download it right now?

      This is a pre-release site. A public release destination has not been confirmed. If you already have a source checkout, the build guide explains the local path. There are no store links or published installers to recommend yet.

      Does it work outside my local network?

      Connections can use direct paths and optional encrypted relays. Availability depends on network conditions and configuration; this is not an always-offline or always-local promise.

      Does support give me a badge or faster support?

      No. Tips buy no digital benefit, priority support, service, ownership, royalties, or revenue share. Optional website acknowledgement is a separate, freely withdrawable choice.

      What happens when I click Ko-fi?

      You leave this site for Ko-fi. Ko-fi and its processors handle checkout and their own cookies, personal data, fees and terms. Peersus never receives your card number. Nothing loads from a payment provider before you choose the link.

      Can I help without paying?

      Absolutely. Reproduce a problem, describe your device and steps, improve documentation, or help someone get started. Remove private paths, device identifiers, logs, and files before sharing a report through the project’s eventual public channels.

      ! DISCLAIMER · READ BEFORE YOU USE PEERSUS

      • You can lose files. Peersus is unfinished. Its data formats and settings can change between versions without notice. Keep a copy of anything you cannot afford to lose.
      • No outside expert has checked it. The connections are encrypted end to end, but nobody independent has audited the code. Do not use it for anything secret. Encryption does not help if you pair a device you should not have trusted.
      • A relay can see that you connected. When two devices cannot reach each other directly, an optional encrypted relay carries the traffic. It cannot read your files. It can see that a connection happened.
      • It is only tested on Linux and Android. Windows and macOS have not been run. The clipboard and cloud upload plugins are not finished; a cloud provider may keep your files in a form it can read.
      • If it breaks, nobody is liable. Peersus is free software under the Apache-2.0 licence, provided as is, without warranty of any kind. Its authors are not responsible for lost data, damage or any other consequence of using it.
      • It is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a replacement for any other file-sharing service, operating system or device maker named or implied on this page.

      What we do not collect

      No account, no analytics, no ads, no cookies. Your settings, your device list and your history stay on your own devices. The only thing that leaves a device is the transfer itself, encrypted. The device you connect to directly sees your network address. An optional relay, when one is needed, sees that a connection happened, between which addresses, and how much data moved. It cannot read the files.
